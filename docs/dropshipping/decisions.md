## Decided

| # | Date | Decision | Why / source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-1 | 2026-10-19 | The supplier ships straight to the customer. The seller never holds stock. | Client chose option 1. |
| D-2 | 2026-10-19 | The supplier or brand name may be shown to customers. A supplier's parcel shows "Shipped by {supplier}". No white-label rules. | Client: "no issue on sharing Supplier or Brand Name". It matches the courier label. |
| D-3 | 2026-10-19 | Sellers bring their own suppliers by invitation. A supplier is visible only to the sellers it is linked to. A public supplier catalogue is Phase 7. | Product owner. |
| D-4 | 2026-10-19 | Every supplier parcel needs a courier and tracking number, sent to the customer the moment it ships. Proof of delivery (a photo) is optional. | Client wants the tracking (POD) number for every parcel, given to the customer in advance. Product owner made proof of delivery optional. |
| D-5 | 2026-10-19 | The seller decides **per supplier** whether cash on delivery is offered, when inviting. Editable later. | Product owner: "make it flexible; the seller tells the vendor whether COD is required". |
| D-6 | 2026-10-19 | Parcel tracking is a **core** feature for every order (`OrderShipment`), not only for dropship. Sellers get it for their own parcels too. | Orders have no tracking field today (`core.prisma`). Tracking belongs to the order the customer sees. |
| D-7 | 2026-10-19 | Invited suppliers need no admin approval. Admins can suspend them. | The seller vouches for a supplier they brought. Config `inviteNeedsAdminApproval` can flip this. |
| D-8 | 2026-10-19 | A supplier is a store: its existing one, or a supply-only store created when it joins. | Reuses the product editor, photos, stock and address. |
| D-9 | 2026-10-19 | The seller's copy of a supplier product is a real `StoreProduct`. | Storefront, cart, checkout, search, SEO and share links work unchanged. |
| D-10 | 2026-10-19 | The customer order is never split. Supplier orders sit beside it. | Checkout and payment code stay unchanged. |
| D-11 | 2026-10-19 | The supplier's stock is the only real stock. It is reserved and released inside the core's own transactions. | No overselling, and nothing depends on an event that could be lost. |
| D-12 | 2026-10-19 | By default the seller's price follows the supplier's price, so their earnings stay the same. It never goes above MRP. The seller can switch to a fixed price. | Sellers never lose money silently, and products don't pause on a price rise. |
| D-13 | 2026-10-19 | Cash on delivery only when the whole order ships as **one parcel**. | The courier collects exactly the order total; no split cash amounts. |
| D-14 | 2026-10-19 | Built as `package/dropship`, the same way as `package/affiliate`: its own schema, no foreign key to core, a host interface, hooks and events. | Keeps core changes small and generic. |
| D-15 | 2026-10-19 | Production stays off (`DROPSHIP_ENABLED=false`) until Phase 5 is done. Phase 0 ships to production on its own. | Phases 2–4 alone would leave sellers with half a feature. |
| D-16 | 2026-10-20 | **Money (was P-1).** Prepaid orders will be split by **Cashfree Easy Split** through PaymentHub: the supplier's share goes to the supplier, the rest to the seller. The in-house payout system is on hold. Until Easy Split is live, money settles **directly** between the seller and the supplier, using the shared statement. COD always settles directly, because the cash never reaches Cashfree. Each supplier order records how it settles (`settleVia`) when it is placed. | Product owner: "we will implement Cashfree split… go with your recommendation… payout system on hold… working with Cashfree to enable it". Cashfree docs checked 20 Oct 2026 ([money](money/context.md)). |
| D-17 | 2026-10-20 | **Returned-parcel fee (was P-2) is configurable per seller–supplier link.** The seller sets who pays the supplier's shipping fee when a parcel comes back (*I pay* / *Supplier bears it*), when inviting. Editable later. | Product owner: sellers may have their own deal with the supplier. |
| D-18 | 2026-10-20 | **Terms are snapshotted at booking.** Placement copies every term onto the supplier order and its lines: prices, shipping fee, COD, returned-parcel fee, ship-within, returns, platform fee, `settleVia`. Every later step reads only that copy, never the current settings. A change affects orders placed after it. Every change is logged. | Product owner: "if the user ordered yesterday, yesterday's fee applies; not the current status". See [terms](terms/context.md). |
| D-19 | 2026-10-20 | A supplier can also pause or end supplying a seller, not only the seller. | Fairness once the seller can change terms (D-17). |
| D-20 | 2026-10-20 | **There is a platform fee** on orders with supplier products (was Q-1). Default: a percentage of the order total, taken from the **seller's** share; the supplier always gets its agreed `supplierShare`. The rate is a config value (Q-5), snapshotted on every supplier order (D-18). It never changes the seller–supplier balance. | Product owner: "yes, there is a platform fee". Whose share and the form (% of total) follow the recommendation the product owner accepted. |
| D-21 | 2026-10-20 | Cashfree holds each vendor's money **12 days** (Defer Settlement) before paying out (was Q-3). | Product owner accepted the suggestion: longest ship-within (7) + typical transit (~5). |
| D-22 | 2026-10-20 | Every store's own payouts move to Easy Split; **each store is a Cashfree vendor** (was Q-4). | Product owner: yes. |
| D-23 | 2026-10-20 | D-16 is read as confirmed: until Easy Split is live, prepaid supplier orders settle **directly** between the seller and the supplier. | Product owner: "yes, implement your suggestion". |
| D-24 | 2026-10-20 | **No guessed tracking links.** A parcel links to its tracking only through a link the seller pasted; otherwise the customer copies the number and opens the courier's own website. The courier list carries websites, not per-number URL templates. | Building Phase 0: none of the couriers' direct tracking URLs could be confirmed on their own sites (JavaScript pages, OTP, captcha) — a wrong link is worse than none. |
| D-25 | 2026-10-20 | **"Came back" doesn't ask about stock.** The parcel's items stay with the order; the seller sends it again (stock untouched) or cancels — and the cancel asks whether the returned items go back in stock. | Building Phase 0: a parcel returned for a wrong address must be sendable again, and asking at cancel time keeps stock from ever being counted twice. Replaces the "restock choice on came back" in the first plan. |
| D-26 | 2026-10-20 | **Pickup is restricted through the `checkout.options` hook**, not a per-product `pickupAvailable` column. The hook answers per cart with the reason the shopper sees, for COD and pickup alike. | Building Phase 1: one seam covers both options and the multi-parcel COD rule (D-13), with no core schema for a package concern. |
| D-27 | 2026-10-20 | **"Send again" makes a new invite link.** The old link stops working and the expiry restarts. | Building Phase 2: only the token's hash is stored, so the old link can't be shown again. "Copy link" is offered right after creating or resending. |
| D-28 | 2026-10-20 | **A supply-only store is marked in the package** (`Supplier.supplyOnly`), not in core. It is created unpublished and without a logo by `createStoreWithoutLogo`; its storefront, marketing and selling rows, the publish card and the launch dashboard are hidden. | Building Phase 2: core `createStore` requires a logo, and a core column would name a package concern. |
| D-29 | 2026-10-20 | **Admin routes live at `/api/v1/admin/dropship`**, not `/api/v1/dropship/admin`. | The admin session cookies are scoped to `/api/v1/admin`; affiliate hit the same problem. |
| D-30 | 2026-10-20 | **The frontend learns the flag from the backend** (`GET /api/v1/dropship/status`, 404 when off). No `VITE_` variable. | One switch per environment; the frontend can't drift from the API. |
| D-31 | 2026-10-20 | **Effects on copies wait for Phase 3**: `codAvailable` re-sync (SU-5), copies pausing on pause / end / suspension (SU-7, SU-8, SU-17), and the "is a copy" refusal (SU-11). Phase 2 offers only the supplier store's own products and leaves one `isCopy()` seam. | Copies (`Resale`) don't exist before Phase 3. |
| D-32 | 2026-10-20 | **"+ New shelf" is the supplier product's own taxonomy category**, created with core `createCategory`, not a typed name. Adding many offers "Each on its own category shelf". A product with no category needs an existing shelf. | Building Phase 3: shelves are taxonomy-backed; a free-typed name has nowhere to go. |
| D-33 | 2026-10-20 | **Delivery-area labels are PIN-code based**: "All India", "Selected PIN codes (N)", "All India except N PIN codes". | Delivery rules hold PIN codes, not states, so "Kerala, Tamil Nadu" can't be worked out. |
| D-34 | 2026-10-20 | **On a copy, the product wizard's Price and Delivery steps are the package's** ("Your price" with the supplier controls; read-only *Set by supplier* delivery and COD). Basics, Photos and Details stay core. The server still refuses locked fields through `product.saving`. | Options, stock and Duplicate all live in the core Price step; swapping the step hides them without touching it. |
| D-35 | 2026-10-20 | **MRP on a copy**: the supplier's MRP caps the price, and is written to the copy's `compareAtPrice` only when above the copy's price. A **%** markup rounds up to a whole rupee. | Core requires MRP > price, so a price at MRP shows no strike-through rather than "0% off"; prices like ₹5,198.70 look wrong to customers. |
| D-36 | 2026-10-21 | **The ordering copy's stock is exact inside the transaction; other sellers' copies re-mirror after commit.** Core takes the copy's own stock, the hook takes the supplier's. Other sellers' copies of that supplier variant follow on `order.placed`, `order.cancelled`, payment and expiry, and the hourly job heals a lost event (C-13). Only the supplier's counter can refuse a sale (K-1, K-2). | Building Phase 4: one counter must be the truth, and a copy's mirror can lag without ever overselling. |
| D-37 | 2026-10-21 | **Admin "force a transition" waits.** Phase 4 has Needs action with Resolve and a note; `POST /supplier-orders/:id/status` stays ☐. Un-shipping a parcel (F-7) stays a manual fix. | A forced status can't safely undo a parcel, stock or a payment, and nothing yet needs it. |
| D-38 | 2026-10-21 | **"You earn" on an order excludes affiliate commission** until Phase 5's statement. It is the lines' total (plus the delivery charge on a supplier-only order, once) − `supplierShare` − `platformFee`. | The package does not read the affiliate package's tables. |
| D-39 | 2026-10-21 | **A supplier parcel needs a real courier and a tracking number**: "Own delivery" isn't offered on the supplier's Ship sheet. On *Came back* the supplier says whether *its* stock goes back; the core cancel then restocks nothing from the parcel (copies mirror the supplier). | D-4: the customer follows the parcel; a supplier ships from its own place. |
| D-40 | 2026-10-21 | **The account is computed on read** from the supplier orders and the live payments, not stored as lines. One pure function decides each line, and both sides call it, so they can never disagree (M-8); a fix to a rule corrects history. Amounts still come only from each order's snapshot (D-18). | Building Phase 5: stored lines would need repair jobs whenever a rule or a status changed. |
| D-41 | 2026-10-21 | **Reminders are tracked by three nullable timestamps on the supplier order** (`confirmRemindedAt`, `lateNotifiedAt`, `deliveredRemindedAt`), claimed in a guarded write, so each goes out once even with two workers. New knob `deliveredReminderDays` (7). | An hourly job must not repeat a reminder, and a timestamp is queryable where an event note is not. |
| D-42 | 2026-10-21 | **The customer-data cut-off stays a read-time rule** (`customerDataDays`, applied when a supplier's order is read). The hourly job has no privacy step. | Already enforced where the data is shown; a job copy of it would add nothing. |
| D-43 | 2026-10-21 | **A supplier's parcel can't be changed from the seller's side.** The core's edit-tracking, delivered and came-back actions refuse a `PACKAGE` parcel (409), and the seller's card hides Edit tracking on it. | Cross-check of Phases 0–5: those core actions had no ownership check, so a seller could move a supplier's parcel and leave the supplier order, its stock and the account out of step. |
| D-44 | 2026-10-21 | **Easy Split is built against a sandbox provider** behind the host (`splitLive`, `getPayoutAccounts`, `requestSplit`, `adjustPayout`), chosen by `DROPSHIP_SPLIT_PROVIDER` (only `sandbox` is built; `paymenthub` is reserved). It records every request in `sandbox_ledger` and moves no money; screens say so. | Product owner: PaymentHub is still being built — "implement Easy Split in sandbox mode, I will integrate the real API later". The package never changes when the host's four methods are replaced. |
| D-45 | 2026-10-21 | **In the sandbox a store's payout account is its existing primary bank account** that hasn't failed verification. The real provider answers from the store's Cashfree vendor (D-22). | The store bank form and table already exist; nothing new to build or retype. |
| D-46 | 2026-10-21 | **Split bookkeeping is on the supplier order** (`splitState`, `splitRef`, `splitAt`, `splitAttempts`, `splitError`, `paidAt`, `feeAdjustedAt`, `feeAdjustError`) and `StatementState` gains `SPLIT`. One request per customer order; its key includes a short hash of the amounts, so a changed split (a supplier falling back) is a new request. | The books must say what happened to each part, whatever the provider. |
| D-47 | 2026-10-21 | **Split timing:** the first try is at least `splitDelayMinutes` (2) after payment (a one-shot timer, plus the hourly job as the safety net); a failure is retried each run; with 2 h left of `splitWindowHours` (24) it goes on admin's Needs action (PS-3). | Cashfree needs about 2 minutes after payment, and the split must land inside the Order Split Delay window. |
| D-48 | 2026-10-21 | **Executing Split Refunds and Cashfree's refund debits wait for the real integration.** Phase 6 shows the per-vendor amounts on *Refunds to make* (PS-8) and records the COD platform fee debit through the provider seam. | They are payment-gateway actions; there is nothing real to execute in the sandbox. |
| D-49 | 2026-10-22 | **A supplier account can never be published.** The core's `setStorePublished` runs a `store.publishing` hook and the package refuses a supply-only store (409); the publish prompt is hidden for it. Seller and supplier stay separate: sellers see a supplier only through the supplier products it offers them; a supplier who also wants to sell to customers creates a store of its own. **Both directions are enforced:** a supplier account is refused on every seller route (`requireSellingStore`, 403; `/role` exempt), and a shop that sells can't join as a supplier (`accept` refuses it, 400; the join sheet lists supplier accounts only). | D-28 said a supply-only store has no storefront, but the "shop isn't live" prompt on its Products page still offered Publish, and one tap put it on the marketplace's "New Stores". |
| D-50 | 2026-10-22 | **The seller's dropship side is two screens, not four.** One **Suppliers** row (tabs *Your suppliers* and *Products to add*, plus an **Add from suppliers** button on Products); the **Shipments** page is removed (each order already shows its supplier's parcel; late and returned parts show on the order and on admin's Needs action); the invite is two short steps (who, then the two terms). A supplier account also loses the customer **Orders** and **Customer messages** rows, and mobile gets a **To ship** tab. | A new seller had to learn four screens for one job, and Shipments repeated the order page. Cut rather than hidden: no unused page, route or endpoint is left behind. |
| D-51 | 2026-10-22 | **An invite is checked against the person who opens it.** A joiner whose account has the invited phone or email is Active at once; anyone else makes the link `PENDING` until the seller approves or declines. A phone or email can have only one open invite per seller. | The link is a bearer secret and the typed name and number weren't verified: a forwarded link made a stranger a supplier at once, and the same number could be invited under many names. Approval keeps joining zero-training for the supplier and puts the decision with the seller. **The approval half is replaced by D-52**; the one-open-invite rule stands. |
| D-52 | 2026-10-26 | **An invite is personal.** Only the account with the invited phone (last ten digits) or email can join; anyone else sees *"This invite isn't for this account"* with the invite's contact half hidden (`98••••••10`) and no join sheet, and `accept` refuses them (403) before anything is created. The invite stays open for the right person. Approve / decline and the link's `PENDING` status are removed; a wrong number is fixed by revoking and inviting again. | The standard for an invite to a named contact (GitHub, Google Workspace, Notion): the seller already said who. Approval asked a non-technical seller a question they shouldn't need, invited a reflexive "Yes" that let a stranger in, and let the wrong person fill in the whole form first. OTP sign-in proves the invited person owns the number, so no fallback is needed. |
| D-53 | 2026-10-27 | **A supplier account adds and offers a product in one flow, in its own words.** The product editor knows it is a supplier account: the price is the **Suggested price**, the delivery step asks only the delivery area, and the last step is **Review & offer** — the price for sellers is asked in place and **Offer to sellers** switches the product on and offers it. Its product list says Offered to sellers / Not offered yet / Paused (switch: Available / Paused), has no home-page placement, and its nav shows no shop-launch "Pending". | The editor said **Publish to my shop** to a supplier who has no shop, and a published product still reached no seller until a second page set its price for sellers — a detour a first-time supplier would not find (zero-training sellers, no detours). |
| D-54 | 2026-10-27 | **"My shops" tells supplier accounts apart, and gives them the supplier's own status.** The package answers `GET /dropship/my-stores` (which of the caller's stores supply, with status, active sellers and orders to ship); the list puts supplier accounts in their own group with **Supplying / Waiting for approval / Paused by UnieMax** and only **Manage**. Core `Store` gains no column (D-28 holds). | Every supplier account showed as an unpublished shop — "Not live yet", "2 of 3 steps done", **Publish** — though it was supplying and can never be published; the owner couldn't tell it from a shop. |
